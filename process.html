How the audit runs.

One structural diagnosis of belief order: what a cold buyer has to believe, in what order, before the price shows up. Not a copy critique, not an A/B program.

What happens

1. Buyer language. Your own research says who buys and why; it comes in as input. Mine reads the other side: how a stranger who hasn’t bought yet files your category. The categories already in their head, the cheaper thing they compare you to, the objections they raise, in their own words from forums and reviews.

You get a map of those categories, in the buyer’s language.

2. Diagnosis. Where exactly the page loses the buyer, traced block by block, with the buyer’s internal monologue at each scroll: which category the price gets judged in, and where the switch happens.

You get a belief-gap map, a cognitive journey map, a synthesis of the dominant conflict.

3. The new order. For every block on the page: what it says now, why it breaks, what takes its place and why. Which existing blocks to rework, which to add, in what sequence. Recorded on video, block by block, with the whole new chain assembled at the end.

You get the restructure blueprint, the conviction architecture, the recorded walkthrough, a one-page strategy summary.

4. Walkthrough. 60–90 minutes with you and whoever owns the page, so the plan becomes your team’s plan.

5. Implementation, with me alongside. Your team builds. I answer the questions the new structure raises and check each block against the plan before it goes live. I don’t write your copy: I set what each block has to say and check what’s written. Within 30 days of go-live, I check how cold visitors move through the new page, on session recordings and funnel data where they exist. If something reads wrong, we fix it together. If the data says the new order isn’t doing what the diagnosis said it would, I re-read the page and correct the plan, inside the engagement.

Timeline

Ten working days from kickoff to walkthrough. Implementation runs on your team’s schedule; I stay with it through go-live and the 30 days after.

Two ways in

On the build, before launch. The audit works as the spec: the diagnosis runs on the current page and your buyers’ own language, so the new page inherits the fixes, not the breaks.

Post-launch, with data. The diagnosis is tested against your own numbers: funnel, scroll depth, session recordings. It works as the post-mortem. The first window simply saves the ad spend that teaches the same lesson.

What it is not

No retainer. No A/B program. No ad creative. No copywriting service: I specify the copy and check it; your team writes it.
